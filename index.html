This is what shaped me

This is not a blog. This is a collection—gathered slowly, with care, like artifacts in a scholar's study. Each piece here has taught me something about being human: how to think more clearly, feel more deeply, or understand the world with a little more patience.

I built this cabinet to remember what matters. Not trends, not noise—just things worth sitting with. Some are essays I've written. Most are works by others that shaped how I see. All of them belong here because they changed something in me.

Welcome. Take your time. There's no rush.

— Uday

[Thought]

Literally Just Do Things

by Erifili Gounari

The simplest advice is often the hardest to follow. This essay reminded me that overthinking is just fear dressed up as strategy. Most of the time, the thing you're avoiding isn't actually hard—you're just scared of what happens if you try. So: literally just do things. Start messy. Start small. Just start.
[Psychology]

The Paradox of Craving to Be Seen But Fearing Being Known

by Harls

We all want to be understood, but we're terrified of being truly seen. This piece named something I'd felt but never articulated: the gap between wanting attention and fearing intimacy. It's easier to perform than to be vulnerable. Easier to curate than to reveal. This essay is a mirror for anyone hiding in plain sight.
[Thought]

We Should All Talk to Strangers

by Angelina Hazzouri

In a world where we've been taught to fear strangers, this essay argues the opposite: talking to people we don't know might be the most human thing we can do. It reminds me that connection isn't just for the people we already love. Sometimes the most meaningful conversations happen with someone you'll never see again.
