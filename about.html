Why I Built This Cabinet

A letter to you, whoever you are

Dear friend,

I built this cabinet because I was tired of forgetting what mattered.

For years, I consumed. Articles, podcasts, videos, tweets. I'd read something brilliant, nod along, feel that spark of recognition—yes, that's exactly it—and then, a week later, it was gone. I'd remember the feeling but not the lesson. The headline but not the insight. The author's name but not what they taught me.

This felt like a kind of betrayal. Not to the writers—they'd done their job. But to myself. I was collecting wisdom like water in a sieve. Nothing stuck. Nothing accumulated. I was learning, but I wasn't changing.

So I started writing notes. Not summaries—marginalia. A few sentences on what a piece taught me, how it connected to something I already knew, what question it left me with. These weren't for anyone else. They were for future me, the one who would forget.

Over time, patterns emerged. Certain themes kept appearing: how to think clearly, how to be patient, how to understand others without losing yourself. Psychology, myth, history, politics—all pointing at the same few truths from different angles.

This cabinet is where I keep those truths. Not as facts to memorize, but as lenses to see through. Each artifact here has earned its place by changing how I think, feel, or act in the world. Some are famous. Some are obscure. A few are my own attempts to articulate what I've learned.

I named it a "cabinet of curiosities" because that's what it is. In the Renaissance, scholars would keep Wunderkammern—rooms filled with strange and wondrous objects. Fossils, maps, instruments, art. Each piece was a doorway to understanding something about the world.

This is my version of that. But instead of fossils, I collect ideas. Instead of maps, I gather perspectives. Each artifact is something that made me stop and think, "Oh. I didn't know that. And now I do."

The goal isn't quantity. I'm not trying to read everything or know everything. I'm trying to understand something deeply enough that it becomes part of how I see. Quality over speed. Depth over breadth. Wisdom over information.

Three values anchor everything here:

  • Deep Understanding — I don't want surface-level takes. I want the kind of insight that reframes how you see a problem. The kind that sticks.
  • Patience — Wisdom isn't fast. It accumulates slowly, through repeated exposure, reflection, and time. I'm not racing to finish anything here.
  • Empathy — Every artifact in this cabinet is about people—how we think, how we hurt, how we grow. Understanding others is the foundation of everything else.

If you care about those things too, you might find something here worth keeping.

A note on curation: I'm picky about what goes in. Not because I think I'm smarter than anyone else, but because this space is sacred to me. I don't add things just because they're popular or because someone told me I should. I add them because they changed me.

Some are essays. Some are books. Some are my own reflections. All of them sit together as equals because, in the end, what matters isn't who wrote it—it's whether it's true, whether it's useful, and whether it makes you more human.

This cabinet will grow slowly. I don't update it on a schedule. I add something when it earns its place. That might be once a month. It might be once a year. I don't know, and I don't mind.

If you've made it this far, thank you for your patience. I hope you find something here that stays with you the way these pieces have stayed with me.

Take your time. There's no rush.

With care,
— Uday
