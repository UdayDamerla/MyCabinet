The Library

Index cards on a shelf. Each artifact is something that taught me how to think, feel, or understand a little better.

[Thought]

Everything is a Win When the Goal is to Experience

by Sentimental Being

This piece shifted how I think about success and failure. If the goal isn't to win but to experience, then nothing is wasted. Every mistake, every detour, every painful lesson—it all counts. It's not about outcomes. It's about what you learn from being alive and paying attention.

[Thought]

Literally Just Do Things

by Erifili Gounari

The simplest advice is often the hardest to follow. This essay reminded me that overthinking is just fear dressed up as strategy. Most of the time, the thing you're avoiding isn't actually hard—you're just scared of what happens if you try. So: literally just do things. Start messy. Start small. Just start.

[Thought]

You Share Other People's Thoughts Because You Don't Have Any of Your Own

by Feifei

This one stung. It's easier to curate than to create. Easier to retweet than to think. I've been guilty of this—filling my brain with other people's ideas and calling it learning. But real thinking is harder. It's sitting with a question long enough to form your own answer. This essay is a wake-up call.

[Thought]

We Should All Talk to Strangers

by Angelina Hazzouri

In a world where we've been taught to fear strangers, this essay argues the opposite: talking to people we don't know might be the most human thing we can do. It reminds me that connection isn't just for the people we already love. Sometimes the most meaningful conversations happen with someone you'll never see again.

[Psychology]

In Order to Love Who You Are, You Must Not Hate the Experiences That Shaped You

by Letters from Rosie

Self-acceptance isn't about erasing your past. It's about integrating it. This piece taught me that you can't love who you are while hating what made you. The painful parts, the embarrassing parts, the parts you wish you could undo—they're all you. And they all count.

[Psychology]

The Paradox of Craving to Be Seen But Fearing Being Known

by Harls

We all want to be understood, but we're terrified of being truly seen. This piece named something I'd felt but never articulated: the gap between wanting attention and fearing intimacy. It's easier to perform than to be vulnerable. Easier to curate than to reveal. This essay is a mirror for anyone hiding in plain sight.

[My Writing]

Invisible Boy: A Memoir of Searching for Connection

by Uday

This is the hardest thing I've ever written, because it's true. For most of my life I was invisible—not by accident, but by design. I cultivated it as armor, then wondered why no one could see me. This is the story of a glass child, the darkness of a coal mine that first made me visible, and the slow realization that connection begins with the courage to be seen.
